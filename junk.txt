Review of lean-CA project.

The findings I would address first are:

1. The generalized-circle preservation theorem does not express the
advertised geometric result.

In ComplexAnalysis/MobiusGeometry.lean:145, isGenCircle_mobiusMap
concludes that there exist coefficients defining a generalized circle
containing one transformed point. Taking all three coefficients to be
zero satisfies this conclusion for every point, independently of the
hypotheses.

There is a second issue: the existential coefficients come after the
particular point z, so the statement does not supply one circle
containing the entire transformed circle.

A suitable statement should choose target coefficients independently
of z, preserve nondegeneracy, and establish the membership relation
uniformly away from the pole. The existing proof contains useful
transformation calculations, but the exported statement loses their
geometric content. The definition’s docstring also needs
qualification: A = 0 gives a line only when B ≠ 0.

2. The spherical-derivative documentation has a factor-of-two error.

ComplexAnalysis/SphericalDerivative.lean:14 identifies sphericalDeriv
with the infinitesimal ratio of chordal distance to Euclidean
distance. However, ComplexAnalysis/ChordalMetric.lean:158 uses the
chordal distance with numerator 2 * ‖z - w‖. Consequently, the
displayed limit is twice sphericalDeriv.

For f z = z at zero, the documented limit is 2, whereas the defined
spherical derivative is 1. The definition and inversion theorem are
consistent; the module and definition docstrings need the
normalization corrected.

3. HasOrderLE is stronger than the conventional meaning of “order at
most ρ.”

ComplexAnalysis/FiniteOrder.lean:47 requires a bound ‖f z‖ ≤ A * exp
(B * ‖z‖ ^ ρ) at the exponent ρ itself. Conventional order is defined
by a limsup, equivalently an infimum of admissible growth
exponents. These differ at the endpoint. See the order and type
definitions in §3.1.

A simple example is f z = z: its conventional order is zero, but
HasOrderLE f 0 would require it to be bounded. I recommend naming the
current predicate as an explicit growth bound, or introducing
conventional order separately and proving the connection. The Hadamard
documentation should distinguish these hypotheses.

4. The advertised Sokhotski–Plemelj result stops short of a
boundary-limit theorem.

ComplexAnalysis/SokhotskiPlemelj.lean:258 calls
cauchyTypeInterior_sub_cauchyTypeExterior the jump relation. Its
statement is an algebraic identity valid for arbitrary coefficients
and points, proved by unfolding the three definitions and ring.

The preceding contour-identification results provide substantial
analytic content. What is missing from the advertised boundary-value
formulation is an explicit continuity or Tendsto result identifying
the two boundary limits. Either add that interface or describe the
current theorem specifically as the identity between the boundary
series.

5. Derivative convergence has avoidable restrictions.

ComplexAnalysis/LocallyUniform.lean:40 specializes to sequences of
scalar-valued functions and concludes pointwise convergence of
iterated derivatives.

The underlying Mathlib theorem
(.lake/packages/mathlib/Mathlib/Analysis/Complex/LocallyUniformLimit.lean:154)
already supports arbitrary filters, complex Banach targets, eventual
holomorphy, and locally uniform convergence of derivatives. An
iterated version in TendstoLocallyUniformlyOn would be a more reusable
primary theorem, with the current statement retained as a corollary.

6. Several other generalizations are worthwhile, without broadening
the whole project indiscriminately.

ComplexAnalysis/AnalyticContinuation.lean:50: the clopen uniqueness
argument can be separated from the unit interval and formulated over a
preconnected parameter space with a chosen starting point. The
endpoint theorem would remain a path specialization.

ComplexAnalysis/Runge/Kernel.lean:66:
IsCompact.exists_finite_measurable_partition is specialized to ℂ,
although its proof uses compact metric covering and measurable-set
arguments. This is a good candidate for general metric/Borel-space
support.

ComplexAnalysis/Harnack.lean:90: an additional version assuming
harmonicity and nonnegativity on the open disc would cover functions
without continuous extension to the outer boundary. The current
boundary version remains useful.

Conversely, Banach-valued Cauchy and residue theory, arbitrary index
types for canonical and Blaschke products, and finite-dimensional
targets for Montel are appropriate choices. Removing Montel’s target
restriction without replacing it by a compactness hypothesis would be
incorrect.
